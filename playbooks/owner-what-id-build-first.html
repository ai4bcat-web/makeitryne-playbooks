What I'd build first in a business you own

The order I'd build AI systems in, inside a business you own, so the work you'd hire for next gets done without the hire.

The rule first

If you build AI for a company you don't own, the leverage goes to whoever owns it. If you build it for something you own, it stays with you.

That's how I went from three trucks to ten without hiring. Not one big system. A few boring ones, built in the right order.

The question I ask isn't "who can I replace." It's "what would I have to hire for next?" Build that instead.

Step zero: the repeat list

Before you touch a tool, spend one week writing down every task that repeats. Yours, your office's, your crew's. Phone notes is fine.

TaskTimes / weekMinutes eachWho does it nowSame steps every time?
[task][your number][your number][name]yes / no
[task][your number][your number][name]yes / no

Times per week × minutes each = minutes you get back. Anything marked "yes" with a big number goes to the top. Anything marked "no" waits.

The order I'd build in

1
Answer every lead in under a minute. Calls, forms, DMs, comments. Every one gets a reply right away, two questions, and a booking link. A lead that waits an hour is a lead your competitor answered.
2
Follow-up that doesn't depend on your memory. Quotes that went quiet, no-shows, past customers. A set sequence goes out on its own until they reply or say stop.
3
The status questions you answer all day. In trucking it's "where's my load." In a gym it's hours and memberships. At a dentist it's appointment changes. Same question, same answer, a hundred times a week. Let a system answer it.
4
Track it to the closed job. Every lead gets a source, and every closed job gets tied back to it. Now you know what's working instead of guessing.
5
Only then, content. More attention into a business that drops leads just drops more leads. Fix one through four, then turn up the volume.
Notice none of these replace the work you're good at. They replace the work that keeps you from it.

Sort your list with Claude

Paste your repeat list into Claude or ChatGPT with this. It does the math and tells you where to start.

I own a [type of business] with [your number] people. Here is every task that repeated in my business this week, with how often it happened, how long it took, and who did it: [paste your list] 1. Multiply times per week by minutes each for every task. 2. Mark which tasks follow the same steps every time. 3. Rank the repeatable ones by minutes saved per week. 4. For the top 3, tell me in plain words what a system would need to do, step by step, and which tools I already use that it would have to connect to. Don't recommend anything that needs a new hire.

This week

  • Monday to Friday: keep the repeat list.
  • Saturday: run the prompt. Circle the top task.
  • Next week: build that one thing. Just one.
