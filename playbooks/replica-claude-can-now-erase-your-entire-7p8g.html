Delete Yourself Off the Internet With Claude

Find every data broker selling your home address and phone number, then get removed from all of them for free. Total cost: one evening and zero dollars.

Step 1

See What's Out There

Open a private browser window and Google your full name in quotes, plus your city. Then run it again with your name plus "address" and once more with your phone number. Private window matters because you want the results a stranger sees, not results personalized to you.

Copy every result that shows your personal info. The site name and the URL is enough. Fair warning: seeing it all in one place is unsettling. That's the point. Now you know exactly what you're cleaning up.

Step 2

Run the Master Prompt

Open Claude at claude.ai and make sure web search is turned on in the settings or the tools menu. Without it, Claude can't look anything up and this whole play falls apart. Then paste this:

The Prompt
Search people-search and data broker sites for my name: [YOUR FULL NAME], in [YOUR CITY, STATE]. I also found these listings myself: [PASTE YOUR GOOGLE RESULTS]. 1. List every site that appears to hold my personal info. 2. Separate actual data brokers from normal accounts I created myself. 3. For each broker, find and link its official opt-out or removal page. 4. Sort the list by how much info each site exposes, worst first.

Claude comes back with the full rundown: the big names like Spokeo, Whitepages, BeenVerified, and Radaris, plus the sketchy clones you've never heard of. For a typical US adult, expect somewhere between 25 and 50 listings. Don't panic at the number. You're about to work the list.

Why the sorting step matters An old forum account you made in college is not a data broker. You delete that one yourself by logging in. The brokers are the ones that scraped you without asking, and those are the ones the law forces to remove you.
Step 3

Generate the Removal Letters

Most brokers have a self-serve opt-out form, and for those you just fill in the form Claude linked. But some make you email them, and some ignore casual requests. That's where the legal letter comes in. Paste this next:

The Prompt
For each broker on the list that requires an email or written request, write me a removal request. Each letter should: 1. Demand deletion of my personal information and opt-out from sale. 2. Cite the California Consumer Privacy Act's right to delete and right to opt out of sale, and note that many brokers honor these requests regardless of the requester's state. 3. Include a line requesting written confirmation of deletion within 45 days. 4. Stay short, firm, and professional. No filler. My info to include: [NAME], [CITY, STATE], and the URL of my listing on that site.

The CCPA is the California law that gives residents the right to have their data deleted, and 45 days is the response window it sets. Many brokers apply one removal process to everyone rather than checking your state, and a growing number of other states have passed similar laws. If you live in California, you have the strongest hand here. Either way, a letter citing specific law gets processed faster than "please remove me."

Step 4

Send Everything and Track It

Now your only job is execution. Work the list top to bottom, worst offenders first. Fill out the opt-out forms, send the letters. Copy, paste, hit send. Most people finish the first pass in about an hour. One episode of TV.

Before you close the tab, have Claude build your tracker:

The Prompt
Turn this list into a tracking table with columns: Site, Opt-out link, Date submitted, Method (form or email), Confirmed removed (Y/N), Re-check date (90 days out). Format it so I can paste it into Google Sheets.

Paste it into a sheet and update it as confirmations come in. Two things to expect: some brokers make you click a confirmation link they email you, and removals take anywhere from a couple of days to a few weeks to actually disappear from search. Both are normal. The tracker is what keeps you from wondering whether you actually finished.

Bonus move Google has a free "Results about you" tool that requests removal of search results exposing your address and phone number. Run it after your broker cleanup so the dead listings drop out of Google faster.
Step 5

Re-Run It Every Quarter

Here's the catch nobody tells you: these sites re-scrape public records. Get removed in January and you can reappear by summer. This is exactly why the paid services charge annually instead of once.

Your fix is a calendar event every 90 days: re-run the Step 2 prompt, compare against your tracker, and opt out of anything that came back. First pass takes an evening. Every pass after takes 15 minutes, because you already have the letters and the links. That's the whole maintenance plan.

The Checklist

  • Google yourself in a private window: name, name + address, phone number

  • Run the master prompt with your name, city, and pasted results

  • Submit every opt-out form and send every letter, worst sites first
  • Build the tracking sheet and log every submission
  • Run Google's "Results about you" tool
  • Set a 90-day calendar reminder and re-run the sweep
